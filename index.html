Pedagogical Guide

Key Concepts & Mathematical Proofs

Essential learning takeaways for machine learning, statistics, and decision analysis students

⚖️ Why Can High Accuracy (99%) Be Catastrophically Misleading? ▼

Suppose you are building an intrusion detection system where true intrusions have a prevalence of 0.5% (5 attacks per 1,000 requests). A naive model that simply never alerts achieves an impressive 99.5% accuracy!

However, its Recall is 0% (all 5 attacks succeed undetected). If the cost of a missed breach is $100,000, that 99.5% accurate model incurs $500,000 in damages. This is why practitioners look at Precision, Recall, Balanced Accuracy, and Expected Misprediction Cost rather than raw accuracy alone.

📐 Mathematical Reconstruction: How 3 Metrics + n Determine the 4 Cells ▼

A 2×2 confusion matrix contains four cells: TP, FP, FN, TN with sum n = TP + FP + FN + TN. Because n fixes one degree of freedom, exactly 3 independent equations are needed to solve for the four cells:

  • Tab 1 Formulation (Precision, Recall, Accuracy):
    P = \frac{n(1 - \text{Accuracy})}{1 + \text{Recall} \cdot \left(\frac{1 - 2\cdot \text{Precision}}{\text{Precision}}\right)}
    Once the actual positive count P is determined, TP = \text{Recall} \cdot P, FN = P - TP, FP = TP \cdot \frac{1 - \text{Precision}}{\text{Precision}}, and TN = n - P - FP.
  • Balanced Accuracy Variant: Since \text{Balanced Accuracy} = (\text{Recall} + \text{Specificity})/2, we immediately have \text{Specificity} = 2 \cdot \text{BalAcc} - \text{Recall}. Then:
    P = \frac{n \cdot \text{Precision} \cdot (1 - \text{Specificity})}{\text{Recall}(1 - \text{Precision}) + \text{Precision}(1 - \text{Specificity})}
  • Prevalence Variant (Bayes' Rule): When Prevalence \text{Prev} is given: P = n \cdot \text{Prev}, N = n(1 - \text{Prev}), directly breaking the degrees of freedom.
🚨 Operational Trade-Off: False Alarms (FP) vs Missed Alerts (FN) ▼

Every classifier has a decision threshold (e.g., probability cutoff p ≥ \theta). Moving this threshold creates an inherent trade-off:

Lowering Threshold (More Eager to Alert)

  • Catches more incidents → Higher Recall (Sensitivity)
  • Fewer Missed Alerts → Lower False Negative cost
  • Triggers more False Alarms → Lower Precision
  • Risk: Analyst alert fatigue, wasted investigation time

Raising Threshold (More Conservative)

  • Alerts only when very confident → Higher Precision
  • Fewer False Alarms → Lower investigation costs
  • Misses subtle incidents → Lower Recall
  • Risk: Severe breach or patient harm from undetected events
